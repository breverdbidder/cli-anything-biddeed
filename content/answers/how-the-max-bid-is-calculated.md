A max bid starts from the after-repair value, discounted for investor margin, then reduced by real repair costs and a buffer for what a listing photo can't show — a survived lien, an unpaid tax certificate, an occupant still living there. The number has to be set before auction day and held to, not recalculated live in the room.

## Start from after-repair value, not asking price

The property's after-repair value — what it sells for once it's fixed up and marketed normally, based on real comparable sales — is the anchor. Everything else in the formula subtracts from that number; nothing in the formula should start from the opening bid or the assessed value, both of which can be misleading signals at a Florida auction.

## Subtract what you can price, then subtract a buffer for what you can't

Real repair costs come first — a number from someone who has priced actual rehabs, not a guess from a listing photo. Then a margin discount, because auction purchases carry more unknowns than a retail purchase. Then a buffer for the things a photo can't show: a lien that survived the sale, an unpaid tax certificate, an occupant still living in the property.

## Write it down before the room gets competitive

The formula only works if you commit to the number before the public sale starts. Recalculating live, under pressure, in a competitive room is exactly how a disciplined max bid turns into an overpay. This is the single number a SIGNAL$ Property Report exists to hand you — pulled from the parcel's actual data, not a guess.
