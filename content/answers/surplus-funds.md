When a Florida auction sells for more than what's owed, the extra money — surplus funds — doesn't go to the buyer. It's held by the clerk and distributed first to lienholders with a claim, then to the prior owner of record, if anyone files a timely claim for it.

## Where the extra money goes

If a property sells for more than the judgment, taxes, and costs it was auctioned to collect, that difference is surplus funds. It belongs to the people with a legal interest in the property before the sale — junior lienholders first, then the owner of record — not to the winning bidder.

## The owner has to claim it

Florida law presumes the owner of record at the time the case was filed is entitled to any leftover surplus, once lienholders with a timely claim are paid. But that presumption isn't automatic money in the mail — someone has to file a claim with the clerk before the deadline, or the funds sit unclaimed.

## Why bidders should still know this

Surplus funds don't change what you paid or what you own — but understanding where the extra money goes explains why a distressed seller sometimes walks away with real proceeds instead of nothing, and why title chains on formerly-foreclosed or formerly-tax-deeded properties can carry claims worth knowing about before you resell.
