A Florida foreclosure sale wipes out the mortgage being foreclosed and any lien recorded after it — but not everything. Property tax liens survive by law no matter when they were recorded, and any lien senior to the foreclosed mortgage survives too. Confirm lien priority before you bid, not after you've won.

## The senior-lien trap

A foreclosure sale only extinguishes what's junior to the loan being foreclosed. A senior mortgage, a senior HOA lien, or a recorded easement that predates the foreclosed loan rides through the sale and becomes yours. This is one of the most expensive mistakes a first-time bidder makes at the courthouse — winning a "good deal" that isn't, because a bigger lien was standing behind it the whole time.

## Property taxes never go away

Delinquent property taxes are different from every other lien. Florida law makes the tax lien a first lien, ranked above every other claim on the property, and that priority doesn't reset because a foreclosure happened. Walk in assuming any unpaid taxes are now your bill.

## Check title before you bid, not after

The only way to know what survives on a specific property is a title search run before auction day — not a guess based on the property's condition or the plaintiff's name. That's what lien-survival classification inside a SIGNAL$ Property Report exists to answer for the parcel you're actually bidding on, pulled the same day, not from memory of "how foreclosures usually work."
