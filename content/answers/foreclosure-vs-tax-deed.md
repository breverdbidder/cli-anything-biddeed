A foreclosure sale enforces a mortgage or a lien and wipes out only what's junior to it — property tax liens and senior liens survive. A tax deed sale enforces unpaid property taxes and wipes out almost everything, with government liens as the one exception. Same courthouse, sometimes the same day, opposite lien-survival rules.

## Two different debts, two different sales

A foreclosure sale exists to collect on a mortgage or a judgment lien. A tax deed sale exists to collect unpaid property taxes. They can run through the same clerk's office, sometimes on the same auction day, and bidders new to Florida routinely confuse which rules apply to which listing.

## What each one wipes out

A foreclosure wipes out the loan being foreclosed and any lien junior to it — a senior mortgage or a property tax lien rides through. A tax deed wipes out nearly everything, mortgages included, with an unpaid governmental lien as the narrow exception.

## Why this matters before you bid

Bidding on a tax deed assuming foreclosure rules — or the reverse — is how a bidder inherits a lien they never priced in. Confirm which sale type you're actually looking at, then confirm what survives that specific sale, before you calculate a number to bid.
