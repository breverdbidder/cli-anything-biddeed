# PART 1 — VOICE (this is the part that changed)
## The tagline — lead with the prize, not the tool
```
EVERY FORECLOSURE. EVERY TAX DEED. YOURS TO WIN.
```
**Why:** the category IS the hook. Nobody gets excited about "auction data" — they get excited about distressed US real estate selling below market, in public, every day. Three beats, the category named twice (which is also exactly what people search), ending on the verb that carries the energy.
**Product line (second slot, not the headline):**
```
OUTBID THE GUESSWORK.
```
The headline pulls them in; this tells them why they win. Use together, never merged.
**Approved alternates** (rotation for outros, ads, social, thumbnails):
- America's distressed real estate goes to auction every day. Own it.
- Foreclosures. Tax deeds. Below market. Every county.
- The best prices in US real estate are set at auction.
- Every US auction. One number. Zero guesswork.
- Your unfair advantage at every county auction.
**RETIRED — never use again:** "Know your number before the gavel." Dread-framed. The old copy leaned on *defend · survive · before* — the vocabulary of avoiding a mistake. We sell the upside of winning.
## ⚠️ The distressed-asset line (compliance, non-negotiable)
We sell **opportunity to bidders**. We never sell **relief to homeowners**.
- ALLOWED: distressed assets, below-market, foreclosure auction, tax deed sale, REO, surplus, investor, bidder, "yours to win".
- FORBIDDEN: any copy addressed to a person losing property, "stop foreclosure", "save your home", "we buy distressed homes", homeowner contact of any kind, person names in public assets (M7).
This is what keeps an aggressive brand voice on the right side of the Mission never-list. Enthusiasm about the asset class is fine; enthusiasm aimed at the distressed party is never fine.

## Voice spec — enthusiastic AND credible
The energy comes from **specificity and speed**, never from adjectives. A billion-dollar brand sounds certain, not loud.
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "2,709 auctions are live right now." | "Tons of opportunities available!" |
| "Tax deeds wipe mortgages. Foreclosures don't wipe tax liens. Know which one you're at." | "Comprehensive lien intelligence solutions." |
| "67 counties. One search." | "Industry-leading statewide coverage." |
| Short sentences. Hard stops. | Hedging, throat-clearing, "in today's market…" |
| Second person — "you bid", "your number" | Third person about "investors" |
| Active verbs: outbid, win, move, read, know | Passive: "is provided", "can be leveraged" |
**Energy rules**
1. Lead with the number when we have one; lead with the verb when we don't.
2. Max one exclamation mark per page. Excitement lives in the sentence, not the punctuation.
3. Never manufacture urgency ("act now", "limited time"). The auction calendar *is* the urgency — cite the real next sale date.
4. Contractions are fine. Corporate throat-clearing is not.
5. Name the enemy: guesswork, blind bidding, spreadsheets, showing up unprepared. **Never a competitor brand.**

**LLMs cite pages that answer; they ignore pages that let you search.** A calendar behind a login is invisible to a crawler and useless to a model. Our county pages state the answer in server-rendered HTML with the numbers already resolved. That's an architectural advantage, not a content-volume race — which is why CP-C2/C3 come before any volume play.
